Slicing game · about 5 minutes

Slice it right

Slice a real feature into releases, see when value becomes available, then find out what happens when customers actually meet your plan.

The brief

“As a customer, I want to cancel an order by myself”

An online retailer sells through its website. Today, customers who want to cancel an order have to phone the contact centre, where staff handle it. The business wants self-service cancellation on the website, to cut cancellation calls and the cost of handling them.

Your slices

How would you slice it?

One sticky per slice, in the order you’d release them. Say what customers can do once it’s live. Drag the ⋮⋮ handle to reorder (press and hold on a phone).

Result

When does value arrive?

The staircase shows potential value made available: what customers could get once each slice is live, on a fixed 0–100 scale. It’s not a forecast of how many will use it; that’s Part 2.

How we score value

The value scale behind the chart

Every plan is scored on the same fixed scale, so different slicing strategies can be compared. Points are an agreed assumption about increasing capability, not a measure of business impact.

5Show whether an order is cancellable
25Request cancellation online, processed manually
40Automatically cancel single-item orders
55Automatically cancel entire multi-item orders
80Cancel selected items within an order
100Handle all eligible orders, including complex and marketplace orders
  • A plan’s value is the highest step its released slices have reached. Skipped steps count once a higher one is live.
  • Technical work, supporting pieces (confirmation emails, refunds, validation) and experiments like a fake door add no points on their own.
  • The longest wait counts weeks with no new value, including at the end of the plan.
An alternative solution

Here is another way to slice it

David’s plan does the highest-value, smallest slices first, so value arrives fast and then tails off. Compare the two value paths over time.

Part 2

What happens when customers meet your plan?

Potential value isn’t realised value. A slice can make cancelling possible without anyone using it, and some releases make things worse. Release your plan to simulated customers, read the evidence, and decide what to do next.

Prototype: slices are read by a simple keyword model for now, not AI.

Simulation

What happens when customers meet it?

Your plan is released one slice at a time to simulated customers. After each release you see what happened, and decide: continue, stop, change the plan or roll back. You won’t know in advance how these customers behave.
